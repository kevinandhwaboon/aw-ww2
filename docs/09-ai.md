## 1. Principles

1. **An AI is a player.** It gets the game state and sends the same commands a human does, through `apply`. It has no special access to the engine and no rule exceptions.
2. **Legal by construction.** The engine rejects anything illegal, so a buggy AI can't break a game. A rejected command is an AI bug, and tests treat it as a failure.
3. **No cheating bonuses.** The first AI gets no extra funds, damage or vision. Difficulty comes later, from smarter play or commander personalities.
4. **Pluggable.** Built-in and community AIs use the same interface, as plain JS/TS modules (00-master-design §15).
5. **Fast enough for theater scale.** The Normandy map will have far more units than today's test maps, so per-unit cost must stay low (section 6).

## 2. The interface

```ts
interface AiPlayer {
  readonly name: string;
  /** The next command for the current player. Called repeatedly until it returns endTurn. */
  chooseCommand(view: AiView): Command | Promise<Command>;
}
```

- **`AiView`** gives the AI the game state with the RNG hidden, the player it controls, and `legalCommands()`. An AI calls the same engine helpers the UI uses (move destinations, attack and fortification targets, forecasts, capture and build checks) on that state.
- **`legalCommands`** (engine) lists every command the engine would accept right now, ending with End Turn, including Carrier launches since 2026-11-13. One simplification: an Unload lists each passenger and tile on its own, not two passengers in one action (D3). The random-playthrough tests send random picks from the same list.
- **It's asynchronous from day one**, even for the built-in AI, so an AI can later run in a Web Worker without changing the interface **(D8)**.
- **One command at a time**, so the UI can animate each move and the state is always valid between calls.
- **Who controls a player** (human or AI) is session setup, not game state. The rules engine doesn't know or care.

## 3. Rules every AI must follow

- **Don't read `state.rng`.** It would let an AI predict every luck roll. Use forecasts, which give the range. `AiView` hides the RNG value so this can't happen by accident.
- **Don't use `Math.random` for decisions.** An AI that needs variety gets its own seeded generator, so AI games replay exactly and bugs can be reproduced.
- **No hidden state between games.** Anything an AI remembers during a game should be rebuildable from the state, so saves keep working.
- **Always finish.** An AI must reach `endTurn`. The session enforces a limit of 300 commands per turn and ends the turn if it's exceeded, logging that as an AI bug. The same happens if an AI throws an error or sends a command the engine rejects.
- **Pure code.** AI code (`src/ai/`) follows the engine's rules: no DOM, no Phaser, no `Math.random`, no clock. The same lint and type checks enforce it.

## 4. The first AI: "Greedy"

Each unit in turn picks its best-scoring action, with no lookahead and no plan across units. That's enough to be a real opponent for testing and an honest baseline to beat.

**Order of play:** units that can attack first, then capturers, then everything else, then Build, then End Turn. Within a group, the most valuable unit goes first.

**Scoring a candidate (destination plus action):**

| Candidate | Score |
|---|---|
| Attack | Value of the damage dealt, minus the value of the expected counter, using the forecast's midpoint. Large bonus for a kill |
| Capture | High, and higher for an HQ, then factories, then cities. Higher still if it finishes the capture |
| Attack a fortification | Only if it blocks the way to an objective |
| Move toward an objective | Closer to the nearest enemy property, or to the nearest enemy unit, is better |
| Retreat | At 3 HP or less, prefer an owned property that repairs this unit |
| Terrain | Small bonus per defense star at the destination |

**Value of damage** = damage % × the target unit's cost. That single rule makes the AI prefer hitting expensive units with cheap ones, without a hand-written matchup table.

**Transport and supply:** in the first version (until §4b is built), Halftracks only carry infantry toward distant objectives and resupply neighbours. Landing Craft and Transport Aircraft are left idle at first, so naval invasions by the AI aren't in the first AI **(D9)**.

**Building:** first ensure a minimum of capture-capable infantry, then spend the rest on the unit type that best counters what the enemy has fielded, scored from the damage chart. Availability and funds limit the choice as usual.

**As built (2026-11-09).** Details the table above left open, all tunable in `src/ai/weights.ts`:

- **Scores are in funds**, the same unit as costs, so an attack, a capture and a step forward compare directly. Attacks use the forecast's midpoint; a sure kill adds half the target's cost, a lucky one 15%. Hitting a unit that is capturing one of our properties adds a bonus.
- **Capture values per action:** HQ 8,000, factory 3,500, airfield and port 3,000, city 2,500. Finishing the capture adds 2,000, and a scenario objective adds 4,000.
- **Where to go:** distances count real movement points over the terrain, so a tank goes round a river instead of getting stuck on the bank. Capturers head for the nearest property not already being captured by a friend, so they spread out. Direct-fire units head for enemies they can damage. Indirect-fire units head for tiles with an enemy in range. Aircraft and ships with nothing to shoot at stay put.
- **Retreat** at 3 HP or less, when out of ammo with no second weapon, or when an aircraft or ship's fuel is within three days' burn of what it takes to get home. It heads for its own property that repairs it. (Since 2026-11-13 the fuel rule also counts the daily burn on the way home, and aircraft count Carriers with room as home: §4b.)
- **Halftracks:** infantry board one when its goal is more than two turns' walk away and the Halftrack hasn't moved yet. Halftracks act last, carry passengers toward properties and unload near them, resupply neighbours, and otherwise stay with the foot units.
- **Building:** at least 3 capture-capable units, or one per three properties still to take, up to 8. After that, every type scores (damage it deals × enemy cost − half the damage it takes × its own cost), averaged over the enemy's units and divided by the square root of its cost, which balances quality against numbers. Before the enemy fields anything, it scores against what they could build. Funds are shared out over the free production sites.
- **Submarines** never dive. Fortifications are attacked only when breaking them shortens the way to the goal by more than two movement points.

## 4a. Greedy v2, part 1: threat-aware production (built 2026-11-13)

Milestone 16. Measured before the change (seed 7, AI vs AI, 40 days): the losing side banked 47,000 to 72,000 unspent funds on Twin Bridges, Bocage Country and Proving Ground, because its own units stood on its factory, which blocks building there. On Normandy one side built 120 Torpedo Boats and the other 84, fighting each other in the Channel while the land war never started. And building only looked at the enemy's whole army, so a Bomber circling the factory counted no more than one at the far end of the map.

The new rules, all tunable in `src/ai/weights.ts`:

- **Spend on the best unit, not the cheapest.** A site builds one unit a turn whatever it costs, so among the units its share of the funds can pay for, the most valuable wins; cheap units only win when money is short (the score is divided by the square root of the larger of the unit's cost and the site's budget, not of the cost alone). Measured before: a side with one factory built 1,000 Infantry every day while 100,000 piled up.
- **Capturers.** Below the minimum of 3 capture-capable units, a land site still builds only those, but the best-scoring one rather than the cheapest. Above it, up to the wanted count (one per three properties still to take, up to 8), capture-capable units get a 1,500 bonus instead of being forced.
- **Keep production sites clear.** A unit ending its move on one of our own factories, airfields or ports loses 2,500 from its score, so it moves off unless it's retreating there for repairs, attacking from there pays clearly better, or it can't move. A freshly built unit is no exception: it steps off next turn.
- **Nearby threats count more.** When choosing what to build at a site, each enemy unit counts by how close it is: 3× when it could reach the site within 2 turns (straight-line distance, not the path, ≤ 2 × its movement + its attack range), 1× otherwise. So a Bomber near the factory makes anti-air the best buy there, and a Heavy Tank on the doorstep makes a Tank Destroyer the best buy.
- **Threats already answered count less.** Each enemy unit's weight is cut by how well our units near it can already hurt it: every one of our units that could reach it within 2 turns covers (its base damage against it × its HP) of it, and the enemy counts only for the part left uncovered. One Mobile AA (75% against a Bomber) leaves a quarter of a Bomber to answer, which can still be worth a second one; two cover it fully, and the next build answers something else. Units built earlier in the same turn count, so one turn's builds don't all answer the same threat. If our units already cover every enemy, coverage is ignored, so building doesn't stop.
- **Only reachable enemies count.** A unit type built at a site scores only against enemies it could get at from there: its movement type can travel from the site to a tile from which the enemy is in its attack range. Aircraft reach everywhere. So a port on a lake doesn't build ships against a fleet in another sea, and a factory on an island doesn't count enemy tanks on the mainland.
- **A threatened site always builds.** Normally a site builds only a unit that scores above zero. When an enemy could reach the site within 2 turns, it builds the best-scoring unit it can afford even if nothing scores well, since a unit in the way beats funds in the bank (it can also block the capture).

Unchanged: the minimum of capture-capable units comes first, and funds are still shared out over the free sites.

## 4b. Greedy v2, part 2: landings, drops and carriers (built 2026-11-13)

Lifts D9. Measured before (2026-11-13, seed 7, 40 days): on The Slot and Normandy neither side ever crossed the water, and on Iwo Jima the US Landing Craft and Carriers never moved. All numbers below are tunable in `src/ai/weights.ts`.

**Stranded units.** A land unit is stranded when its landmass holds nothing for it: no property it could take and no enemy land unit it can hurt, while some other landmass does. Enemy ships offshore don't count. Halftracks never count: they stay behind. A unit at 3 HP or less retreats for repairs first, as before.

**Landing Craft.**

- An empty craft sails to a pickup tile: the free Beach or friendly or neutral Port that costs it and the nearest stranded unit the fewest turns to reach, taken together. It waits there.
- A stranded unit heads for the nearest craft with room that stands where it can load, or for the pickup tile an empty craft is sailing to, and boards as soon as it can reach the craft. It keeps off the pickup tile itself until the craft is there.
- A craft with one passenger waits for a second while any stranded unit could reach it within 2 turns. Then it leaves.
- It sails for the Beach or Port from which its passenger has the shortest way to its goal (a property to take, or else enemy land units and properties), counting both the sailing and the walk, and unloads **both passengers in one action** (D3).
- A craft with passengers presses on while it has the fuel to land them and still get back to one of our ports. Otherwise the usual fuel rule decides (below). Pickup tiles in a port count one turn dearer than a beach, since a craft waiting in a port keeps our ships from refuelling there.

**Transport Aircraft.** The same, for foot soldiers: it waits on our own airfield, takes stranded units aboard, flies to a tile beside the passenger's goal and drops it, right onto the property when that tile is free. It only drops within one turn's walk of the goal.

**Carriers.**

- **Launch:** an aircraft takes off only to attack, and only when its best move from the Carrier is an attack worth making. Aboard, it's refuelled, rearmed and repaired free, so otherwise it stays. Launches come before any other move in the turn.
- **Sailing:** a Carrier moves or launches, not both. With aircraft aboard and nothing for them to hit, it sails to within their flying range of a target and no closer than 2 tiles less. Ending where an enemy that can hurt it could attack next turn costs 3,000.
- **Landing:** an aircraft heading home for fuel, ammo or repairs counts our Carriers with room as home, alongside airfields. An aircraft with nothing to do lands on a Carrier it can reach rather than circling.

**Fuel.** Two changes for every aircraft and ship, since part 2 sends them further from home.

- **Heading home** now counts the daily burn for the days the trip takes, not only the distance: a unit turns back once its fuel is under (distance home + (days home + 3) × daily burn). The old rule left the days out, so it fired too late on long trips.
- **The point of no return:** a move that would leave less fuel than the trip home from the new tile needs costs 5,000, so a unit doesn't step past it except for a very valuable attack. A unit that couldn't get home even from where it stands is exempt.

These help less than hoped. On The Slot over 60 days, both sides together lost 21 and 24 Torpedo Boats to empty tanks in two games before the rules, and 5 to 21 over four games after (6 or 7 with the part 1 AI, which sailed less). **Open problem:** most of those boats do turn for home in time and still run dry. The suspected cause is our own ports being occupied by freshly built ships when they arrive, but two fixes for that (moving units off production sites first, and not building where a unit is coming in) changed nothing measurable and were dropped.

**Building.**

- **Landing Craft:** a port builds one while we have more than 2 stranded units per craft we own, up to 6 craft, and the craft could reach them. **Transport Aircraft:** an airfield builds one when stranded infantry can walk to it and we have none.
- While a transport is wanted but too dear, its cost is kept back: the other sites share only what's left, so the funds build up.
- **An army to ship:** a land unit type built on a landmass with a Beach or Port counts enemies it could only reach by sea at half weight (it counted them at nothing in §4a), provided we own a Landing Craft or could build one.
- Unarmed units (Halftrack, Landing Craft, Transport Aircraft, Carrier) are never chosen by the general scoring. The AI builds transports only by the rules above, and never builds Halftracks or Carriers.

**The engine's command list** now includes every legal launch (`launchCommands`), so "listed means accepted" covers Carriers too, and the random playthroughs exercise them.

**Measured after** (40 days, seeds 1 to 5 on every map, no AI bugs in any game): 41 of 55 games end with a winner, against 43 after §4a. The difference is Contested Skies, where 2 of 5 end within 40 days, down from 4; every other map is unchanged.

- **Iwo Jima:** the US launches its Dive Bombers (9 or 10 launches a game) and ferries reinforcements from its base island (3 or 4 landings). Japan still holds out the 15 days every time.
- **The Slot:** each side makes roughly 20 to 30 landings and 7 to 21 launches a game, and islands change hands (typically 8 properties against 17 by day 40, where before it stayed 10 against 10). No game on seeds 1 to 5 ends within 40 days. Run to 80 days on seeds 1 to 5 and 7, four of six end (days 34, 48, 54 and 64). The two sides together lose 21 to 32 Landing Craft in combat over 60 days, about half of them with troops aboard.
- **Normandy:** the Allies build Landing Craft and Transport Aircraft and land a few squads, but German ships sink most craft in the Channel. No winner by day 40.

So the planned test, "every built-in map ends with a winner within 40 days", is **not met** for The Slot and Normandy. What's missing is in §8:

- **Massed landings.** Craft leave two units at a time, and on the enemy's home island the factory replaces losses as fast as they arrive.
- **Escorts.** Craft sail alone through enemy Torpedo Boats and Destroyers.
- **Garrisons.** Every stranded unit leaves, so a home island can be left empty.
- **Fuel at sea.** Ships still run dry in numbers (above).

## 5. In the UI

- A human vs AI game is chosen on the map select screen, per player: a "Computer plays …" box for each. By default the computer plays everyone but the first player. The URL can say who it plays: `?ai=1`, `?ai=0,1` (watch AI vs AI) or `?ai=none`.
- During an AI turn, input is locked and its commands animate like a human's, with a short pause between units. A speed option can come later.
- The pass-the-device screen is skipped for AI players, and shown only when two or more people play.
- During an AI turn you can still move the cursor and peek at units, but not give orders, end the turn or undo.
- The debug panel's event log shows what the AI did.

## 6. Performance

- Target: an AI turn on today's maps feels instant apart from animation, and thinking takes under 100 ms per unit. Measured on 2026-11-09: under 1 ms per command on the small maps, about 8 ms on Normandy. A test keeps it under 100 ms.
- Reachable tiles are already bounded by each unit's move points, so cost grows with unit count, not map area.
- Scoring must avoid comparing every unit with every other unit on theater maps. Group enemies by area if that becomes a problem. This is measured, not guessed: a benchmark test will track it.

## 7. Testing

- AI vs AI games, using the random-playthrough invariants already in the test suite.
- Every command the AI sends is accepted.
- Games between AIs finish within 40 days on every built-in map where the sides can reach each other by land. On the maps that need landings (D9), AIs play 10 days without an AI bug. Measured on 2026-11-09: Twin Bridges, Bocage Country, Crossroads, Bastogne, Iwo Jima and Proving Ground all end with a winner; Narrow Sea, Beachhead, Normandy and Contested Skies stall. Measured again on 2026-11-13, 40 days, seeds 1 to 5 on every map: before §4a, 38 of 55 games ended with a winner (Narrow Sea, Normandy and The Slot never; Twin Bridges 3 of 5); after §4a, 43 of 55 (Narrow Sea 4 of 5, Twin Bridges 5 of 5, Contested Skies 4 of 5; Normandy and The Slot still never, as they need §4b). No AI bugs in any game. After §4b: 41 of 55; see §4b for Contested Skies, The Slot and Normandy.
- Spot tests: it takes a free kill, it captures an undefended HQ, it doesn't attack when the trade is clearly bad.
- Same seed, same game: AI vs AI replays exactly.
